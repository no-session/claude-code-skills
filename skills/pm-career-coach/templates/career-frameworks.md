# PM Career Frameworks

## Shreyas Doshi's Frameworks

### Good PMs vs Great PMs

| Dimension | Good PM | Great PM |
|---|---|---|
| Decisions | Metrics-driven | Metrics-**informed** (blend quant + qual + intuition) |
| Customer insight | Listen to what customers say | Listen to what customers **don't** say |
| Team dynamics | Inspire with their own ideas | Facilitate teams to generate ideas collaboratively |
| Feedback | Ask "Does this make sense?" | Ask "What am I getting wrong?" |
| Focus | Problem solving | **Problem prevention** |
| Strategy | Execute strategy flawlessly | Ensure the **strategy itself is optimal** before executing |

Use this to diagnose: is the person solving problems or preventing them? Are they executing someone else's strategy or shaping the strategy itself?

### The LNO Effectiveness Framework

All PM tasks fall into three categories:

- **Leverage (L):** 10x-100x return on effort. Put maximum effort here. Examples: strategy docs, key hiring decisions, critical stakeholder meetings, product vision.
- **Neutral (N):** Standard return. Do these competently but don't over-invest. Examples: meeting notes, routine status updates, standard sprint planning.
- **Overhead (O):** Must be done but minimal quality needed. Examples: admin tasks, expense reports, status reports nobody reads.

**The counterintuitive insight:** "Actively try to do a bad job on certain tasks." Most PMs burn out because they put A+ effort into everything. Great PMs put A+ into L tasks, B into N tasks, and C into O tasks.

**Career coaching application:** When reviewing someone's resume or hearing about their work, classify their bullets as L, N, or O. A resume full of N and O work ("managed backlog," "facilitated standups," "wrote status reports") signals someone who isn't prioritizing Leverage work.

### 10-30-50 PM Senses Framework

Three essential PM senses:
1. **Execution Sense:** Align people toward objectives, orchestrate complex projects, ship reliably
2. **Analytical Sense:** Frame right questions, evaluate from multiple facets, simulate outcomes, use data well
3. **Product Sense:** Intuition for what users need, design taste, creative problem-solving

**Target:** Top 10% in one sense, Top 30% in another, Top 50% in the third.

Nobody is top 10% in all three. Knowing your distribution helps you:
- Choose roles that leverage your top sense
- Partner with people who complement your weaknesses
- Focus development on moving from 50th to 30th percentile (highest ROI)

### Career Decision Frameworks

**Title-Money-Scope:** When evaluating a job, you're optimizing across three competing dimensions. You rarely get all three. Know which one you're prioritizing and why.

**Tablestakes-Ego-Joy:** Tablestakes = minimum requirements (comp, location, role type). Ego = external validation (title, brand, prestige). Joy = intrinsic satisfaction (the work itself, the people, the mission). Most career regret comes from optimizing for Ego over Joy.

**3-Year Lighthouse Goal:** Set a directional target for where you want to be in 3 years. Not a rigid plan — a lighthouse that guides daily decisions. "In 3 years, I want to be leading product for a growth-stage AI company" shapes every job application, side project, and networking decision.

### High-Agency PM Concept

Great PMs have strong internal locus of control. They don't accept "that's how we do things" — they edit company processes and product ethos.

**Diagnostic question:** When this person hits an obstacle, do they find a way around it or escalate it? High-agency PMs find a way. They treat constraints as design parameters, not blockers.

## Jackie Bavaro's Frameworks

### Three Phases of a PM Career

1. **Phase 1: Shipping Products** (Early PM) — Ship products that delight customers and hit goals. Focus on execution, reliability, and earning trust. Success = things ship on time, work well, and customers use them.

2. **Phase 2: Product Strategy** (Senior PM) — Identify new opportunities and come up with winning strategies. You start telling the company where to focus, not being told. Success = you define the roadmap, not just execute it.

3. **Phase 3: Organizational Excellence** (Leadership) — Build teams and systems, not just products. Success = your team ships great products without you in the room.

**Coaching application:** Diagnose which phase someone is in and whether their resume/goals match. A Phase 1 PM trying to talk about strategy on their resume sounds hollow. A Phase 2 PM's resume should scream strategic ownership.

### Five PM Skill Categories

1. **Product Skills:** User research, data analysis, design sense, technical understanding
2. **Execution Skills:** Project management, MVPs, scoping, launches, time management
3. **Strategic Skills:** Vision, roadmaps, business models, OKRs, goal-setting
4. **Leadership Skills:** Communication, collaboration, mentoring, mindsets
5. **People Management:** Recruiting, coaching, performance management, team org

Use this to identify skill gaps. Most career transitions into PM over-index on Execution and under-index on Product and Strategic skills.

### Career Growth Formula

**Advancement = Scope + Autonomy + Impact** (not skill level alone)

You can be highly skilled and not advance if you don't demonstrate autonomy at increasing scope with measurable impact. The formula is multiplicative — zero in any dimension means zero advancement.

### 8 Paths Into PM

1. **APM programs** — Requires CS background, top school, leadership evidence, high GPA
2. **Internal transfer** — Take on PM-like tasks alongside current work, then request title change
3. **Big Tech → Startup** — Use brand name + PM-adjacent experience to get first PM title at a startup
4. **MBA** — Provides network and structured transition, but avoid appearing arrogant or treating PM as stepping stone
5. **Specialized expertise** — Deep domain knowledge (healthcare, finance, legal) makes you uniquely qualified for industry-specific PM roles
6. **Networking** — 60% of PM hires come through network referrals
7. **Cold outreach** — Write product analyses as a power user, not a tourist
8. **Founder of acquired startup** — Entrepreneurial experience is the strongest PM signal

## Sachin Rekhi's Frameworks

### 5 Paths to First PM Role

1. **CS Graduate → APM:** Top school, strong GPA, leadership evidence, analytical fundamentals. Target: Google, Microsoft, Meta, LinkedIn APM programs.
2. **Engineering + MBA:** Combine technical foundation with business acumen. Need compelling transition narrative and entrepreneurial side projects.
3. **Adjacent Role Transfer:** From design, engineering, marketing, support, sales, or ops. Write specs, run point on projects. Internal transfers are far easier — "the team knows you well."
4. **Entrepreneur:** Document product leadership rigorously. Show PM best practices, not just "flying by the seat of your pants."
5. **Domain Expert:** Build personal brand through blogging, speaking, industry events. Deep understanding of technology applied to your domain.

### Three Dimensions of PM Advancement

1. **Independence:** Supervision needed → fully autonomous → guiding others' autonomy
2. **Product Scope:** Feature enhancement → feature area → full product → product suite
3. **Leadership:** Feature team communication → cross-team → cross-org → executive → board

## Marty Cagan's Product Operating Model

**The Build Trap (Melissa Perri's term for what Cagan fights against):** Teams that are feature factories — measured by output (features shipped) instead of outcomes (problems solved). PMs in build traps are project managers with a different title.

**Empowered Teams (Cagan's ideal):** Teams given problems to solve, not features to build. PMs in empowered teams own outcomes. They have the skills, authority, and accountability to discover and deliver solutions.

**Career implication:** When evaluating a company, ask: "Are PMs given problems or features?" If features, you'll grow execution skills but not strategic skills. This is the single most important question for PM career development.

## Teresa Torres: Continuous Discovery Habits

**Opportunity Solution Tree:** A visual framework connecting desired outcomes → opportunities (user needs/pain points) → solutions → experiments. Forces PMs to think in terms of the problem space before jumping to solutions.

**Career implication:** PMs who practice continuous discovery — weekly customer touchpoints, rapid experimentation, evidence-based decisions — develop Product Sense faster than those who rely on intuition or stakeholder requests.

## PM Specializations (2026 Market)

| Specialization | Focus | Skills Premium | Demand |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AI PM** | Products powered by ML/AI, LLMs, generative AI | 15-20% | Highest — hockey stick growth |
| **Growth PM** | Acquisition, activation, retention, monetization | 10-15% | High — every company needs growth |
| **Platform PM** | Internal tools, APIs, developer experience | 10-15% | High — infrastructure companies |
| **Core/Feature PM** | Vertical product slices, user-facing features | Baseline | Steady — always in demand |
| **Technical PM** | Execution-heavy, deep eng collaboration | 5-10% | Moderate |
| **Data PM** | Data products, analytics, data infrastructure | 10-15% | Growing |

**Positioning advice:** Don't call yourself a "generalist PM" — it signals nothing. Pick the specialization that matches your strongest experience and the market you're targeting. You can be a "Growth PM with AI experience" or a "Platform PM who's shipped developer tools." Specificity wins.
