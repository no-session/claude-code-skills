# PM Resume Review Rubric

## Overview

This rubric combines Ken Norton's six hiring criteria (from "How to Hire a Product Manager") with Aakash Gupta's resume failure taxonomy. Use it to systematically evaluate a PM resume, then deliver findings as narrative feedback.

## Scoring Dimensions

### Dimension 1: Shipped Products (Ken Norton)

Norton's strongest filter: "Unless the position is very junior, strongly prefer candidates who've shipped products from concept to launch."

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 0 | No evidence of shipping. Resume reads like project management or analysis. |
| 1 | Mentions launches but vaguely — "supported launch of X" or "contributed to Y." |
| 2 | Clear ownership of shipped features with some context on scope and impact. |
| 3 | Multiple shipped products with full lifecycle evidence (discovery → launch → iteration) and measurable outcomes. |

**What to look for:** Action verbs like "defined," "shipped," "launched," "owned." Red flags: "assisted," "supported," "helped," "participated in."

### Dimension 2: Impact & Metrics (Aakash Gupta)

Gupta's core resume principle: pair input metrics (what you built) with output metrics (what happened). Resumes with 3+ quantified achievements get 40% more interviews.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 0 | No metrics anywhere. Pure responsibility descriptions. |
| 1 | A few metrics, but vague ("improved engagement") or irrelevant to PM role. |
| 2 | Most bullets have metrics. Mix of input (features built, users served) and output (revenue, retention, conversion). |
| 3 | Every bullet follows XYZ: "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]." Metrics are specific, relevant, and impressive. |

**The XYZ Method:**
- Before: "Managed product roadmap for enterprise platform"
- After: "Defined and shipped enterprise SSO integration that closed 12 blocked deals ($2.4M ARR), reducing sales cycle by 3 weeks"

### Dimension 3: Product Specificity (Aakash Gupta)

Gupta's #1 resume failure: sounding like a product analyst instead of a product manager.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 0 | Could be any role — project manager, business analyst, consultant. No product names, no decisions made. |
| 1 | Mentions products but describes process, not decisions. "Conducted user research" without what it led to. |
| 2 | Names specific products/features and describes PM-specific decisions (prioritization, trade-offs, scoping). |
| 3 | Crystal clear what products they owned, what decisions they made, and why those decisions mattered. Reader can picture the product. |

**Red flags:** "B2C SaaS product" (name it), "cross-functional team" without saying what was built, "identified opportunities" without what was shipped.

### Dimension 4: Leadership Without Authority (Ken Norton)

Norton: "PMs lead by influence, not authority. Prioritize reference checks from peers and ICs rather than direct reports."

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 0 | No evidence of leadership. Reads as an individual contributor in a support role. |
| 1 | Mentions working with teams but no evidence of driving decisions or alignment. |
| 2 | Clear examples of aligning stakeholders, making trade-off decisions, or driving cross-functional outcomes. |
| 3 | Demonstrates leading through ambiguity — resolved conflicts, said no to stakeholders, built consensus on controversial decisions. |

### Dimension 5: Technical Credibility (Ken Norton)

Norton: "I'd take a wickedly smart, inexperienced PM over one of average intellect and years of experience."

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 0 | No technical signal. Could be a non-technical PM at any company. |
| 1 | Lists tools (JIRA, Figma) but no evidence of technical depth. |
| 2 | Shows technical context — API integrations, data pipelines, ML models, architecture decisions. |
| 3 | Demonstrates technical depth that earned engineering trust. Talks about systems, not just features. |

### Dimension 6: Strategic Thinking (Lenny Rachitsky)

Lenny's Senior PM differentiator #1: "Develop and evangelize strategies leading to meaningful impact."

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 0 | All tactical — task execution with no evidence of strategic input. |
| 1 | Mentions "strategy" or "vision" but abstractly, without substance. |
| 2 | Shows evidence of setting direction — defined roadmap themes, identified new opportunities, influenced product strategy. |
| 3 | Clear strategic arc — identified problem space, defined approach, executed, measured, iterated. Shows thinking, not just doing. |

## Aakash Gupta's Three Failure Categories

After scoring dimensions, check for these systemic issues:

### 1. Product Specificity Issues
- Sounds like a product analyst, not a PM
- Uses generic terms ("B2C SaaS product") instead of naming products
- Describes what was found, not what was shipped
- Lists cross-functional team sizes instead of direct reports

### 2. Vagueness Problems
- Filler words: "highly accomplished," "effective communication," "results-driven"
- 30,000-foot descriptions with no ground-level detail
- Missing context for lesser-known companies (add revenue/size/industry)
- No explanation of the "so what" — why did this work matter?

### 3. Information Organization
- Strongest accomplishments not in top-left position (where eyes scan first)
- Metrics irrelevant to target role (cost metrics when applying for growth role)
- No clear personal brand or through-line across experiences
- Too much space on old/irrelevant roles

## ATS Readiness Check

75% of PM resumes get filtered by ATS for lacking keywords. Check for:

- [ ] "Roadmap" appears at least once
- [ ] "User research" or "customer discovery" present
- [ ] "A/B testing" or "experimentation" present
- [ ] "Cross-functional" present
- [ ] Product-specific keywords matching target job description
- [ ] Standard section headers (not creative titles)
- [ ] No tables, graphics, or multi-column layouts
- [ ] Single-column, top-to-bottom format
- [ ] Simple fonts (Arial, Calibri)

## Formatting Check

- [ ] 1 page if under 8 years experience, 2 pages max for 8+
- [ ] Half-inch margins
- [ ] 5-7 bullets for recent roles, 3-5 for older
- [ ] Each bullet under 2 lines
- [ ] Contact info: name, location, email, LinkedIn
- [ ] Professional summary (skip if under 3 years experience)

## Delivering the Review

**Do NOT present a score table.** Internalize the rubric and deliver findings as narrative coaching feedback:

1. **Open with the biggest gap** — the one thing that would most improve their hit rate
2. **Call out what's working** — be specific about strengths
3. **Rewrite 2-3 bullets** to demonstrate the XYZ method
4. **Give a clear verdict** — would this resume get past screening at their target company type?
5. **Provide a prioritized fix list** — what to fix first, second, third

**Example output tone:**

"The strongest signal on your resume is the Checkout experience work — it's specific, metric-driven, and clearly PM work. But your top three bullets all describe process ('conducted research,' 'facilitated workshops,' 'managed backlog') instead of outcomes. A hiring manager scanning this for 6 seconds — which is what Ken Norton describes as the real filter — would see a project manager, not a product manager. Let me show you how to fix your lead bullet..."
