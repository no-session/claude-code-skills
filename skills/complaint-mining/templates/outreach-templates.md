# Outreach Templates

The complainers are your distribution. They've publicly self-identified as buyers for a better version. DM them.

Realistic numbers: 30-50% reply rate, 10-20% become interview/test prospects, 5-10% convert to paying customers.

## Universal rules

1. **Reference the specific complaint they posted.** Not "I saw your review" — quote the actual phrase.
2. **Don't pitch in the first message.** Ask for a 15-min chat or offer free beta access.
3. **Keep it under 80 words.** Long DMs = unread.
4. **Give them an out.** "No worries if not your thing" — reduces anxiety, increases reply rate.
5. **One ask per message.** Either chat OR test access, not both.
6. **Sign with your real name + one credible signal.** "I'm building [thing] solo" or "Used to work at [company]."

## By platform

### Reddit DM

Reddit users are skeptical of DMs. Lead with their post.

```
Subject: your post about [topic]

Hey [username],

Saw your post in r/[sub] about [the specific complaint, paraphrased
in 5-10 words]. I'm building a small alternative that fixes exactly
that — solo, indie, not a startup.

Would you be open to 15 min on a call so I can make sure I'm building
what you actually want? No sales pitch, just questions.

Either way, thanks for the post — it's exactly the kind of feedback
incumbents ignore.

— [Your name]
[optional: 1-line credibility — twitter handle, prior company, etc.]
```

**Why this works:** specific, humble, asks for advice not a sale, 80 words.

---

### Reddit comment reply (better than DM if their post is recent)

If the original complaint is <30 days old, replying publicly often beats DMing — other complainers see the reply and self-recruit.

```
This was the exact pain that made me start building [name]. I'd love
to see what you'd do differently — DM me if you'd be open to 15 min,
or just reply with whatever broke your patience.
```

**Variant when soft-launching:**

```
Yeah — I'm 4 weeks into building a tiny tool that exists because of
exactly this complaint. If you want early access (free), DM me.
Mostly looking for honest "this doesn't work" feedback.
```

---

### Twitter / X DM

Twitter users tolerate DMs better than Reddit. Still keep it short.

```
Hey [name] —

Saw your tweet [link or direct quote] about [the pain]. Quit using
[incumbent] for the same reason. Building a small alternative right
now (solo, no funding).

Would you be open to a 15-min call as I build? Looking for 5 honest
testers from people who actually quit, not just complained.

Either way — your tweet is in my "north-star quotes" doc.

— [Your name] / @[your_handle]
```

---

### Twitter / X public reply

Same logic as Reddit — sometimes more powerful than DM because it surfaces to other complainers.

```
Same. Building [tiny tool] because of this. Free beta if you want it.
DM open.
```

(Yes, that's the entire reply. Brevity is the point.)

### App Store / Play Store reviewers

App Store reviewers don't have a DM channel — but they often have:
- A display name that's their real name or a Twitter handle
- A reviewer profile (App Store) showing other apps they've reviewed

**Workflow:**
1. Pull the reviewer's display name from your scrape
2. Search Twitter, LinkedIn, Reddit for the same handle
3. If you find them on another platform, message there using one of the templates above
4. Always reference the original review explicitly: "Found you via your App Store review of [app]"

**Conversion is lower** (5-10% reply vs. 30%+ on Reddit) because they didn't expect to be findable. But the signal is much higher — these are people with real installed app evidence.

### LinkedIn (for G2 / Capterra B2B reviewers)

B2B reviewers usually have public LinkedIn. InMail or connect-then-message.

```
Subject: your G2 review of [product]

Hi [first name],

Found your G2 review of [product] — specifically the part about
[the specific pain]. We had the same problem at [your previous
context, if relevant], which is why I'm building [wedge].

I'm interviewing 10 people this month who've felt this same pain
to make sure I'm building the right thing. Would you be open to
20 minutes? Happy to send a Calendly link or work async via email
if easier.

Either way, thank you for the honest review — they're rare in our
category.

[Your name]
[Title / context]
```

**Connect first or InMail directly?**
- Connect-first: higher acceptance, but slower; use when you have time
- InMail (if you have premium): faster but lower reply rate; use when launching soon

---

### YouTube comment authors

YouTube comments are harder to DM directly. Go via:
- Channel "About" page → email if listed
- Channel handle → search Twitter / LinkedIn
- If they're a creator with a podcast/blog → reach out through that

```
Hey — saw your comment under [video] about [pain]. I'm building [thing]
solo because of that exact frustration. Would love your perspective on
whether I'm solving the right problem. 15 min call?

[Your name]
[Where to verify you're real — Twitter, GitHub, prior company]
```

## Templates by ask

### Ask 1: 15-min interview (highest information density)

Use when: you're still validating, haven't built yet.

```
[opening tied to their complaint]

I'm building a small fix for this. Before I commit, I'm interviewing
10 people who've felt the pain — not a sales pitch, just questions
about how you currently work around it.

15 min, async if you prefer. Open?
```

### Ask 2: Free beta access (faster, lower-quality signal)

Use when: you have a working prototype or concierge MVP.

```
[opening tied to their complaint]

I built a tiny fix for that exact thing. Want free access? Looking
for honest "this still sucks" feedback — not paying users yet.
```

### Ask 3: Paid pilot (highest signal, hardest to land)

Use when: you have a concierge MVP and want WTP evidence before building software.

```
[opening tied to their complaint]

I'm building a fix manually for 5 people right now to prove the
concept before writing software. $[X] for a 30-day pilot — full
refund if it doesn't help. Interested?
```

## Volume targets

For a wedge to clear the validation bar, send roughly:

| Goal | Outreach volume | Realistic outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 5 Mom Test interviews | 30-50 DMs | 15-20 replies, 8-10 booked, 5 done |
| 3 paid concierge pilots | 50-100 DMs | 20-30 interested, 5-7 quoted, 3 paid |
| 50 paying customers post-launch | 200-400 DMs + community posts | 50 in the first 90 days |

If your reply rate is <15%, the message is wrong (probably too long or too pitchy). Iterate the template, not the volume.

## Common DM failure modes

- **Generic opener.** "I noticed you use [app]" — not specific enough. Always quote the actual complaint.
- **Pitch in line 1.** "I'm building [name], an AI-powered [category]…" — instant delete.
- **Multi-paragraph essay.** Anything past 100 words gets skimmed. Cut it.
- **Asking for a Zoom call out of nowhere.** Offer async first if Zoom feels like a lift.
- **No personal signature.** Anonymous "Founder, [App]" gets ignored. Use your name.
- **Following up too fast.** If no reply in 5 days, send one short bump. After that, leave them alone — they'll see your launch.

The first 20 DMs you send will be bad. The 21st will be good. Send the first 20 anyway.
