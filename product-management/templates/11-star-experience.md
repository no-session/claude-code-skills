# 11-Star Experience

Our vision for the ideal user experience, inspired by Brian Chesky's framework from Airbnb.

---

## The Framework

The 11-star exercise pushes us beyond incremental improvement to imagine truly transformative experiences. We rate our product experience from 1 star (broken) to 11 stars (physically impossible but inspiring). The magic happens in the gap between "industry standard" (5 stars) and "our target" (6-7 stars) — that's where differentiation lives.

The goal isn't to build an 11-star experience. It's to stretch our imagination past what feels reasonable, then find the highest-impact ideas that are actually feasible.

---

## How to Run an 11-Star Exercise

### Step 1: Pick a specific user journey
Don't try to rate the "whole product." Pick one concrete flow — e.g., "A new user sets up their first project" or "A PM writes and gets approval on a PRD."

### Step 2: Rate each star level
Walk through what each star level looks like for that specific journey. Be vivid and specific — the exercise works best when you can *feel* the difference between each level.

### Step 3: Identify the sweet spot
Your target typically lives at 6-7 stars. That's where experiences feel magical but are technically and economically feasible.

### Step 4: Work backwards
From your target star level, identify the specific capabilities, designs, and technical investments needed to get there.

## Tips for Running a Great 11-Star Session

1. **Do it as a team.** Include eng, design, PM, and ideally someone from customer-facing roles. Different perspectives make the exercise richer.
2. **Be specific.** "The onboarding is great" is useless. "The user creates their first project in under 2 minutes without reading any docs" is useful.
3. **Don't censor the high stars.** The crazier the 9-11 star ideas, the better. They often contain kernels of genuinely shippable innovation.
4. **Use it for roadmap prioritization.** "This feature moves us from 4-star to 6-star on our core workflow" is a powerful way to frame investment decisions.
5. **Revisit quarterly.** Your star ratings will (should) change as you ship improvements and as customer expectations evolve.

---

*This framework is a thinking tool, not a scoring system. The value is in the conversation and the ideas it generates, not in the numbers themselves.*
